# Run your SA business from your phone: quotes, stock and cashflow

*Originally published at [https://vlyt.app/blog/run-sa-business-from-phone/](https://vlyt.app/blog/run-sa-business-from-phone/).*

Most South African solo operators do not lose money because they lack a desktop suite. They lose money because the **price, the quote, the stock count and the month** live in different places — WhatsApp, a notebook, a spreadsheet, and memory.

This guide is the pillar for that problem. It explains how to run the catalog-to-quote-to-stock loop from a phone, when a spreadsheet starts hurting you, and how the related Vlyt Learn guides fit together.

## What “phone-first” actually means

Phone-first does not mean “screenshot a spreadsheet.” It means the **source of truth** for products, customers, quotes and stock is an app you open between jobs — not a file that only works on a laptop.

A workable phone system covers four jobs:

1. **Catalog** — products, variations, packaging, ZAR prices you trust.
2. **Quotes** — line items pulled from that catalog, with VAT where it belongs.
3. **Stock** — enough visibility that you do not promise what you cannot deliver.
4. **Cashflow** — income, expenses and money still waiting, for the current period.

If any one of those still lives only in chat history, you will retype numbers and argue about which version was current.

## The week before a proper system

Typical pattern for builders, electricians, plumbers, landscapers and small traders:

- Prices get typed into WhatsApp, then copied into a sheet — nobody is sure which is current.
- Stock is a notebook or a rough mental count until a customer is already waiting.
- Expenses sit in one place, sales in another, and “how did we do?” in a third.

That is not laziness. It is what happens when tools assume you have an office admin. For a deeper breakdown of spreadsheet quoting failure modes, read [why spreadsheet quoting breaks down](https://vlyt.app/blog/spreadsheet-quoting-problems/).

## Build the loop in three steps

### 1. Add a catalog and a few contacts

Capture the products and services you actually sell, with the price you want to quote — not a round number you invent in the chat. Add the people you sell to often. This is the foundation for every later quote.

See [how to build a product catalog for quote-based businesses](https://vlyt.app/blog/product-catalog-quote-businesses/).

### 2. Build quotes from real prices

A professional quote should pull line items from the catalog so you are not reinventing the total under pressure. Include VAT clearly when it applies. Then send it without rewriting the maths in WhatsApp.

## What good looks like on a phone screen

| Job | Weak setup | Strong setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Typed fresh in every chat | Saved once on the product |
| Quote | Paragraph in WhatsApp | Structured lines + total + VAT |
| Stock | Find out when the customer waits | Level visible on the item |

You do not need SARS-grade books on day one. You need **one number the customer pays** and **one view of whether that number made the month work**.

## Choosing a tool (without the hype)

When you compare apps, ignore feature grids that assume a five-person office. Score tools on:

- Setup time for your real catalog
- ZAR and VAT behaviour on quotes
- WhatsApp-friendly sharing (PDF or clear text without retyping)
- Stock visibility if you hold inventory
- Whether you can see cashflow without exporting to Excel

## Common mistakes

- Treating WhatsApp as the archive of prices.
- Quoting without knowing if the item is still in stock.
- Mixing quote language with invoice language (customers get confused; so does your follow-up).
- Waiting for a “perfect” desktop setup before capturing today’s prices.

## FAQ

### Do I need a laptop to run quotes and stock properly?

No. Many SA solo operators run catalog, quotes, stock checks and a simple month view from a phone. You need one system that keeps prices and quantities together — not three apps plus a notebook.

### Is WhatsApp enough as a business system?

WhatsApp is excellent for sending. It is a weak place to store prices, VAT lines and stock. Keep the numbers in a catalog or quote tool, then share the finished quote in chat.

### What should live on the phone first?

Start with products and prices, then contacts, then quotes. Add stock and expenses once quotes are consistent. Cashflow on Home only helps if the quote and expense data is already trustworthy.

### How is this different from Sage or a card machine?

Desktop accounting and card machines solve books and payments. Phone-first tools like Vlyt solve the day-to-day loop of catalog to quote to stock for people who sell and dispatch themselves.

### Can quotes include South African VAT?

Yes — if your tool supports VAT on line items or quote totals in ZAR. Always state whether the number the customer sees is inclusive or exclusive before you send.

### Where should I start this week?

Capture ten real products with prices, write one clean quote from that list, and stop retyping amounts into WhatsApp. Then track whether those items are still in stock.
