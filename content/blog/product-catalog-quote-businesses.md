If the catalog is wrong, every quote is wrong. For quote-based South African businesses, the catalog is not a nice-to-have web shop — it is the **price memory** of the company.

Part of [running the business from your phone](/blog/run-sa-business-from-phone.html).

## Direct answer

Build a catalog of the products and services you actually sell, with ZAR prices you trust. Build every quote from those lines. Update the catalog when suppliers change — not only the last WhatsApp message.

## What to store per item

- Clear name (customer language, not only supplier codes)
- Unit (each, m, kg, hour)
- Sell price in ZAR
- Optional cost (for your margin sense)
- Variations / packaging if price or stock differs
- Stock quantity if you hold inventory ([stock guide](/blog/stock-tracking-phone-traders.html))

## Catalog hygiene rules

1. One item, one meaning — do not create “Cable”, “cable”, and “CAB-6” as three truths.
2. Retire dead items instead of leaving ghost prices.
3. Review top movers monthly when copper, cement or imports move.
4. Never let a one-off chat discount silently become the new list price without a decision.

## From catalog to quote

With a clean list, [writing a professional quote](/blog/how-to-write-professional-quote-south-africa.html) is assembly, not invention. That is how you stay fast on site.

## Shipping and packaging notes

If you sell packed goods, store pack size on the item so you do not quote single-unit prices for bulk packs by mistake.
