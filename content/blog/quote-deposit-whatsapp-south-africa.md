A WhatsApp **“yes”** feels like a win. It is not a booking until money or a clear deposit rule is in place.

This spoke supports [running your SA business from your phone](/blog/run-sa-business-from-phone/). It sits next to [quote vs invoice](/blog/quote-vs-invoice-south-africa/), [sending quotes on WhatsApp without losing the numbers](/blog/send-quote-whatsapp-without-losing-numbers/), and [cashflow basics for trades](/blog/cashflow-basics-trades-sa/).

## Direct answer

Put **deposit rules on the quote** before you send it. When they accept, collect the deposit (or written acceptance that matches your rule) **before** materials leave or you block the week. A chat “yes” with no payment is still pipeline — not income.

## Why “yes” burns cash

Typical solo-operator week in South Africa:

- You send a clean quote on WhatsApp.
- They reply “yes please” or “go ahead.”
- You buy tile / cable / timber the same afternoon.
- The deposit never lands. The job slips. Stock sits. Margin is gone.

The failure was not the quote total. It was treating **enthusiasm as commitment**.

## Quote language that sets the rule

Your [professional quote](/blog/how-to-write-professional-quote-south-africa/) should already have a next step. Make the deposit impossible to miss:

| On the quote | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Deposit amount or % | 40% deposit before materials are ordered |
| What “booked” means | Diary slot held when deposit clears |
| Validity | Quote valid until Friday — deposit still required after accept |
| How to pay | Bank details / reference = quote number |

Cover message on WhatsApp can repeat the one line that matters:

> Total R12,480 incl VAT, valid Friday. Reply yes and I’ll send deposit details — materials leave after the deposit clears.

Do not invent a new total in the thread. If they negotiate, [edit and resend](/blog/send-quote-whatsapp-without-losing-numbers/).

## Acceptance vs booked vs paid

| State | What happened | Your move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | No clear accept | Follow up or wait ([open quotes](/blog/follow-up-open-quotes-south-africa/)) |
| Accepted / “yes” | They want the job | Send deposit invoice / pay request |
| Deposit paid | Money in for the hold | Order materials, lock the date |
| Awaiting balance | Work done / goods out | Invoice the rest |
| Paid in full | Job closed | Income bucket |

Keep **accepted** and **deposit paid** as separate statuses in your head (and in your tool). Mixing them is how you spend money you do not have — see [cashflow basics](/blog/cashflow-basics-trades-sa/).

## Deposit invoice, not a fake “quote”

When they say yes:

1. Open the **saved** quote — same totals.
2. Send a short **deposit request** (invoice or payment note): amount, bank details, reference = quote number.
3. Log when it lands.
4. Only then order stock or travel.

A [quote is an offer; an invoice asks for money](/blog/quote-vs-invoice-south-africa/). Do not stamp “INVOICE” on the same PDF you used as a quote to sound softer — it confuses VAT, disputes, and your own follow-up.

## Message templates

**After they say yes**

> Thanks Thabo — locking this in with a R4,992 deposit (40%). Banking details below; please use reference BL-1048. I’ll order materials once it shows.

**If they stall after yes**

> Happy to keep the quote open until Friday. I can’t hold materials or the Saturday slot without the deposit.

**If they want work before deposit**

> I can start once the deposit clears. If cash is tight we can trim scope on an updated quote — I won’t buy stock on a chat yes alone.

## Habits that protect the week

- Never buy non-returnable materials on a verbal or chat yes alone.
- Put the deposit % on every quote template so you are not negotiating from scratch.
- Friday check: accepted-but-unpaid deposits — nudge once, then free the diary.
- Cap how many “yes, deposit pending” jobs you carry; each one is a soft promise to your future self.
